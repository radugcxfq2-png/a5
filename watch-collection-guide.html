The Collecting Guide

A collection is not a number. It is a small group of watches that each have a reason to be there and all get worn. Here is a calm way to build one.

01Begin with roles, not models

Before looking at any watch, write down the situations in your life that call for one: work, weekends, swimming, travel, formal occasions. Then ask which of those your current watch handles badly. That gap is your next watch. Collecting by role prevents the most common mistake, which is owning four watches that all do the same job.

02The three-watch idea

For most people, three roles cover everything.

  • An everyday watch. Comfortable, legible, water-resistant enough for rain and hand washing, neutral enough for any clothes.
  • A robust watch. At least 100 m of water resistance, strong lume and a strap that can get wet. This is the one for the beach, the garden and the gym.
  • A dress watch. Thin, simple and quiet, on leather.

One versatile watch can fill two of these roles. Many people stop at two and are entirely content.

03Vary one thing at a time

If you add a fourth or fifth, make it different in a way you can feel: a different dial colour, a different complication, a different case shape or a different strap material. Two steel watches with black dials will always compete for the same days, and one of them will lose.

04Store them properly

Watches not being worn should rest somewhere dry, cool and away from direct sun. A lined box or a drawer with a soft insert is ideal. Keep them apart so cases and bracelets do not rub.

  • Lay them dial up or on their side, crown upward.
  • Keep them away from speakers, tablet covers and handbag clasps, which contain magnets.
  • Push crowns in fully before putting a watch away.
  • Let leather straps dry completely before closing them in a box.

05Rotate with intention

A mechanical watch is happiest when it runs regularly. Its oils stay distributed and you notice early if something changes. Wearing each one for at least a day every couple of weeks is plenty. Quartz watches need no exercise, but check them monthly so a dying battery is caught before it leaks.

The Rotation Planner works out a simple schedule for the number of watches you own.

06Keep simple records

For each watch, note the model reference, the date you got it, the date of its last battery change or service, and where the papers and spare links are kept. A single page or a note on your phone is enough. It saves a great deal of searching when a watch needs attention.

07Know when to stop

The sign of a finished collection is that nothing is neglected. If a watch has not been worn for a year, it is not part of your collection in any real sense. Pass it to someone who will wear it, and enjoy the ones that remain a little more.
